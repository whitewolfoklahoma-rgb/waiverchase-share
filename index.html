      State-form library

      Twelve states prescribe statutory waiver forms; the rest use custom forms. This demo ships the three states Redbud works in. WaiverChase picks the form from the job's state automatically — the packet always carries the form name so anyone can check it.

      California — statutory forms (Civil Code §§ 8100–8150)
      • Conditional Waiver and Release on Progress Payment — Civil Code §8132
      • Unconditional Waiver and Release on Progress Payment — Civil Code §8134
      • Conditional Waiver and Release on Final Payment — Civil Code §8136
      • Unconditional Waiver and Release on Final Payment — Civil Code §8138

      Use the statutory wording exactly; a non-statutory form risks being unenforceable in California. Notarization is not generally required for California waivers.

      Texas — statutory forms (Property Code Chapter 53, Subchapter L)
      • Conditional Waiver and Release on Progress Payment — statutory form, Tex. Prop. Code §53.283
      • Unconditional Waiver and Release on Progress Payment — statutory form, Tex. Prop. Code §53.284
      • Conditional Waiver and Release on Final Payment — statutory form under Subchapter L
      • Unconditional Waiver and Release on Final Payment — statutory form under Subchapter L

      Texas requires the statutory forms for waivers to be effective. Texas waivers generally must be notarized if they are to be recorded or relied on by lenders — finals are flagged in the packet.

      Oklahoma — no statutory form
      • Conditional Waiver and Release on Progress Payment — custom form (Oklahoma prescribes no statutory waiver form; lien law is Title 42)
      • Unconditional Waiver and Release on Progress Payment — custom form
      • Conditional Waiver and Release on Final Payment — custom form
      • Unconditional Waiver and Release on Final Payment — custom form

      Because Oklahoma has no statutory form, the wording follows the widely used AIA-style custom forms. Have your attorney review your template once — then WaiverChase fills it forever.

      Help

      The short version of everything WaiverChase does.

      How do I enter a pay app?

      On the Pay Apps tab, pick the job, give the pay-app number and period, and type each sub's amount for the period. Save once — WaiverChase generates a waiver for every sub with an amount, on the correct form for the job's state.

      How do subs sign and return a waiver?

      Open the Waiver Packet for the pay app and mark it sent — each sub's waiver carries a no-login sign-and-return link, so the sub opens it, signs, and it comes back. Nobody creates an account. The Chase Queue holds the pre-written reminder email for anyone who hasn't.

      What do the statuses mean?

      Generated — the waiver exists but hasn't been sent. Sent — it's with the sub. Chased — at least one reminder has gone out. Received — the signed waiver is in hand and logged. Only Received counts toward a job's completeness bar.

      How does the chasing work?

      The Chase Queue lists every outstanding waiver, finals and big dollars first, with the email already written and the sign link inside it. The ladder runs at 3, 7, and 14 days after sending. Mark a chase done and the card re-sorts; mark the waiver received and it leaves the queue and lands in the Received Log.

      What does "matched" mean in the Received Log?

      When a signed waiver is logged, WaiverChase checks it against its pay app: same sub, same form type, same amount, same through-date. A waiver for the wrong amount is flagged as a mismatch and does not cover the payment — the original waiver stays in the chase queue until the right one arrives.

      Where does my data live? How do I back it up?

      In this demo edition, everything lives on this device, in this browser — no account, nothing uploaded. Export the waiver log CSV from the Dashboard to keep a dated copy, and use Reset below to restore the fictional sample data or Clear to empty the app. Clearing your browser's site data erases your records, so export before you do.
